Forecasts of glucose 60 minutes ahead from continuous glucose monitors (CGM) can warn people with type 1 diabetes before glucose becomes dangerously low or high. A forecast is more useful when it comes with a prediction interval, or band, that states how far it can be trusted. Bands are usually judged by their average coverage, the share of true values that fall inside them, and an average can hide failures in particular situations. This paper asks whether a nominal 80\% band around a 60-minute forecast stays honest in each situation. A situation is the current glucose level (one of five ranges, from below 70 to above 250 mg/dL) combined with how much glucose changed over the previous 30 minutes (one of five categories, from falling fast to rising fast), which gives 25 situations. Per-patient forecasters were trained for 215 people from 11 public datasets, and eight of the datasets (153 patients) were held out to test the methods only after they were designed. With the test forecasts of all datasets combined, a standard GARCH band covered between 62\% and 91\% of outcomes depending on the situation. A situation-aware band, which learns a separate width for each situation, kept coverage between 72\% and 82\% and reduced per-situation miscalibration in all eight held-out datasets while producing narrower bands.
